The 3 dB/oct bell

A broad move.
A deliberate shoulder.

Sometimes a tone needs a long, gentle gesture. Choose the nominal 3 dB/oct bell, the open 6 dB/oct contour, or a higher-order shoulder. The shape changes at the same Q, giving you another decision alongside frequency, gain and width.

3 dB/oct6 dB/oct12 dB/oct24 dB/oct96 dB/oct

Actual filter responses: 1 kHz, +12 dB, Q 0.7071, 48 kHz. Bell slope labels describe nominal finite-gain shoulder steepness. A bell returns toward unity; it is not an infinite stopband.

Thirteen families. Proprietary SIMLAF character.

The curve has
a character of its own.

Precision is the reference. British, USA, German and SIMLAF families introduce gain-dependent widths, different boost/cut behaviour and compound shoulders. Choose how the move gathers, leans and settles around its centre.

Precision · The neutral family keeps the core shape as your reference.

Actual response at 1 kHz, +12 dB, Q 0.7071, 24 dB/oct and 48 kHz. Regional names describe our original design families; they do not claim a replica of an unnamed piece of hardware.

Precision

A clear starting point

The neutral family keeps the core shape as your reference before you choose a more characterful contour.

British I

A bandwidth that follows the move

Gain-dependent width changes how a broad tonal move concentrates as the gain increases.

British II

Different gestures for boost and cut

Asymmetric width and an opposing lower shoulder make adding presence and taking it away distinct decisions.

British III

A lift with an upper counter-shape

Gain-dependent width meets a higher opposing shoulder for a shaped presence move.

USA I

A gain-dependent focus

The bandwidth changes with gain, giving small and assertive moves different proportions.

USA II

Two sides of the control

Boost and cut use different widths, changing the character as well as the sign of the move.

USA III

A compound contour

German I

A more focused starting contour

A tighter bandwidth gives the family a different concentration from Precision at the same displayed Q.

German II

Broad body and a defined upper edge

A wider central shape is paired with an opposing upper contour.

German III

A move with a lower foundation

SIMLAF Crown

A centre framed by its shoulders

Opposing contours on both sides frame the central peak or dip.

SIMLAF Orbit

An asymmetric orbit around the centre

A lower counter-shape and upper supporting contour create an intentionally asymmetric response.

SIMLAF Velvet

A broad, gain-dependent gesture

A generous core and a softer upper counter-shape give small and larger moves different proportions.

Five more ways to shape the tone.

Baxandall Bass / Treble

Broad, gradual tone-control gestures, with the hinge set by Frequency.

Silk Air

A broad upper lift with a shaped lower shoulder. Explore air while controlling the edge beneath it.

Pillow Low

Low-end weight with the region above it held back, giving body and the low mids separate roles.

Ear Glare

An asymmetric presence-region dip. Shape the sensitive region and the shoulders around it together.

Harmonic EQ · Up to eight related copies per band

Move the fundamental.
Carry the decision through.

Take 500 Hz down and carry that move into its related partials. Set the number of copies, spacing, interval and depth. Use integer multiples or logarithmic intervals, taper the higher moves, or alternate boost and cut with signed depth.

Fundamental onlyFour related copies

Actual response: 500 Hz, −6 dB, Q 3, 12 dB/oct, integer spacing, 12-semitone interval, 80% depth, 48 kHz. Harmonic EQ adjusts partials already present in the recording; it does not create saturation harmonics.

Per-band spectral dynamics

Let the resonance move.
Let its neighbour stay.

A conventional dynamic band follows a shared detector. Spectral mode gives frequencies inside the selected supported gain band their own detectors, so a loud resonance can trigger a different correction from the quieter sound beside it. External sidechain and mid/side routing remain part of the decision.

−10.37 dBHot 1 kHz component in the documented fixture
≈ 0 dBQuiet 1.35 kHz neighbour in the same fixture
Read the condition behind the result

48 kHz; input amplitudes 0.4 at 1 kHz and 0.01 at 1.35 kHz; band centre 1 kHz, Q 0.7071, range −18 dB, threshold −24 dB, ratio 4:1, attack 5 ms and release 80 ms. These are recorded processor-integration results for this two-tone fixture, not a promise of arbitrary sub-bin separation.

At 48 kHz, spectral mode reports 4,096 samples / 85.33 ms of delay and 11.72 Hz bin spacing. Transform size scales with sample rate; finite frequency resolution and attack/release remain part of the sound. Dynamic cuts use whole-band cutoff movement; spectral operation applies to supported gain-bearing shapes.

A different relationship between bands

Build the tone
in parallel.

Serial.

The result of one band feeds the next. Their changes accumulate through the chain.

Parallel.

Each branch receives the original signal. Its contribution joins the mix with phase preserved, giving overlapping moves a different relationship.

The graph follows the routing.

Left, right, mid and side choices share the same complex response used by the audio path, graph totals and gain estimation.

Keep the mode deliberate.

Parallel and spectral operation use minimum-phase static filters. The saved linear-phase preference returns when those modes are disengaged.

The workspace belongs to the curve

The graph fills the panel.
The controls come to the band.

Floating Curve, Dynamics and Harmonics tabs leave the graph available. Choose display range directly on the graph; ±30 dB is the default. New nodes remember the selected slope. Daylight and Dark keep the same normal interface as the window changes size.

Inside SIMLAF EQ

The complete working vocabulary.

01ShapeA filter for the decision+
  • 20 independently placed bands.
  • 14 filter types, including five broad tone shapes.
  • 10 nominal slope choices on supported shapes: 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 48, 72 and 96 dB/oct.
  • Gain from −30 to +30 dB; Q from 0.025 to 40.
  • Frequency control spans 10 Hz–30 kHz; usable response depends on sample rate.
02CharacterOriginal families and house curves+
  • Precision plus three British, three USA, three German and three SIMLAF families.
  • Gain-dependent bandwidth and asymmetric boost/cut behaviour by family.
  • Compound shoulders that change the contour around the centre.
  • Baxandall Bass and Baxandall Treble.
  • Silk Air, Pillow Low and Ear Glare.
03Gentle bellsA different kind of broad move+
  • Nominal 3 dB/oct bell shoulder option.
  • 6 dB/oct bell for another broad contour.
  • Higher-order bell shoulders change shape at the same Q.
  • New nodes inherit the selected or last-used slope.
  • Nominal bell slopes describe finite-gain shoulders, rather than infinite rejection.
04Related partialsCarry a move through the harmonic structure+
  • Up to eight additional related-frequency copies on supported gain shapes.
  • Integer-multiple or logarithmic interval spacing.
  • Interval from 3 to 24 semitones.
  • Signed depth can alternate boost and cut between copies.
  • Copies taper toward Nyquist and are excluded above the supported high-frequency boundary.
05Band dynamicsMake the move respond to level+
  • Dynamics on supported gain shapes and low/high cuts.
  • Above- or below-threshold operation.
  • Dynamic range from −30 to +30 dB.
  • Ratio from 1:1 to 100:1; threshold from −80 to 0 dB.
  • Attack from 0.1–500 ms; release from 5–5,000 ms.
06Spectral dynamicsLet frequencies inside the band respond independently+
  • Independent frequency-bin detectors within the selected supported gain band.
  • External sidechain detection.
  • Stereo and mid/side routing.
  • Static IIR response retained when spectral movement is neutral.
  • At 48 kHz: 4,096-sample reported delay, 85.33 ms and 11.72 Hz bin spacing.
07TopologyChoose how the bands meet+
  • Serial processing cascades the band responses.
  • Parallel branches each receive the original signal.
  • Parallel response combines complex phase, rather than adding dB magnitudes.
  • Per-band stereo, left, right, mid and side placement.
  • Graph totals and automatic gain estimation follow the routed response.
08Cuts and phaseWork with the appropriate processing mode+
  • Low/high-cut families with damping differences at supported orders.
  • Level-driven dynamic cuts move their cutoff.
  • Minimum-phase operation has zero reported latency by default.
  • Optional linear phase when compatible with the active modes.
  • Parallel and spectral operation use minimum-phase static filters; the saved linear-phase choice returns afterwards.
09WorkspaceKeep the curve in view+
  • Full graph with transparent floating band controls.
  • Curve, Dynamics and Harmonics control tabs.
  • Display range lives on the graph and defaults to ±30 dB.
  • Daylight default and Dark option.
  • A single normal interface across 40%–400% window scale.
10Session workflowRecall the decisions+
  • Input, output and sidechain analysis views.
  • Band enable and solo, global bypass and output trim.
  • A/B comparison, undo/redo and preset save.
  • Overall gain scaling and automatic gain compensation.
  • Existing parameter IDs and original slope automation positions retained; appended fields migrate to neutral in older states.
