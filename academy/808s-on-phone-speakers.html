Lesson 04 · Low end

Making 808s and bass translate to phone speakers

A phone can't play your 808's fundamental, but it can play its harmonics, and the ear fills in the rest. How to give it the right ones.

By SIMLAF Audio9 November 20266 min read

  2. The missing fundamental
  3. Start with the note and the sound
  4. Four ways to add the right harmonics
  5. Built into TECTON 2 and hVOLT
  6. Check it like a listener

Most people will hear your track for the first time on a phone. The 808 that shakes the studio can vanish on a phone speaker, leaving a kick, some hats and a hole where the bassline was. That is not a mixing mistake. It is physics: a speaker the size of a fingernail cannot move enough air to play 40 Hz. You can't fix the speaker. You can give it something it can play.

What a phone can play

Small drivers in small boxes roll off steeply in the low end. Most phone speakers give you very little below about 300 Hz, and many roll off higher still; laptop speakers usually give up somewhere around 150–250 Hz. An 808 usually sits between 30 and 70 Hz, well below both.

The lowest harmonic of each note that a speaker starting at 300 Hz can play.
NoteFundamentalLowest harmonic above 300 Hz
E141 Hz8th, 330 Hz
G149 Hz7th, 343 Hz
A155 Hz6th, 330 Hz
C265 Hz5th, 327 Hz

So on a phone, the 808 is its harmonics, or it is nothing.

The missing fundamental

Play the harmonics of a note without the note itself (100, 150, 200 and 250 Hz, but no 50 Hz) and you still hear a pitch of 50 Hz. Your brain works out the fundamental from the spacing of the harmonics. This is the missing-fundamental effect, and it is the reason anyone can follow a bassline on a phone at all.

That sets the job: make sure every note of the 808 has harmonics where a small speaker can play them, consistently, without turning it into a buzz on a big system. A clean, sine-like 808 has almost no harmonics, which is exactly why the long, smooth 808 that sounds huge in the studio is the first thing to disappear.

You can't make a phone play 40 Hz. You can make the listener hear it.

Start with the note and the sound

Processing can only bring out harmonics the 808 can carry, so three choices come before any plug-in:

  • Tune it. An out-of-tune 808 fights the chords in exactly the range a phone plays, because that is where its harmonics land. Check the root note against the key with a tuner.
  • Mind the octave. The same bassline an octave up gives a phone more to work with: E2 reaches 330 Hz with its fourth harmonic, while E1 needs its eighth. If the low notes vanish and the higher ones survive, the octave is part of the answer.
  • Keep some attack. The first few milliseconds of an 808, its click or its pitch drop, carry energy well above the fundamental. A soft, slow start gives a small speaker nothing to grab.

Four ways to add the right harmonics

Saturation

Soft clipping and tube-style saturation add harmonics of the note. Symmetrical clipping adds mostly odd harmonics, which sound grittier; asymmetrical saturation adds even ones as well, which sound warmer. In SIMLAF Drive, switch on Two Bands with the crossover around 150–250 Hz, drive the low band and turn High Drive down to 0 dB (it starts at 9 dB); Auto Gain, on by default, keeps the comparison honest. Drive until you can follow the notes on small speakers, then back off a little. Two cautions:

  • Saturate the 808 on its own, not on a bus with the kick. When two unrelated sounds go through the same distortion, you also get intermodulation: new tones at the sums and differences of their frequencies, which belong to neither sound.
  • Match the loudness when you compare. Saturation adds level, and louder always seems better.

Parallel, band-limited distortion

Send the 808 to a parallel channel, distort it hard, then high-pass the distorted copy at about 150–200 Hz and blend it in underneath the clean 808. The fundamental stays clean on big systems, and the phone gets its harmonics. Of the four, this is the easiest to control.

An exciter, after the saturation

An exciter builds new harmonics from what is already above its frequency setting, so it cannot work on a pure 40 Hz sine. It earns its place after saturation, or on an 808 that already has some upper harmonics: it adds more of them, cleanly. SIMLAF Exciter generates nothing from content below its Frequency control, which goes down to 100 Hz, and it adds true harmonics of the notes already playing, without the intermodulation tones between notes that make excited bass sound dirty. Set Frequency between 100 and 200 Hz, switch on Listen to Harmonics to hear only what it adds, and set the amount with that in your ears.

Virtual bass

Virtual bass goes one step further: it generates the harmonic series of the low band specifically so the ear infers the fundamental, and lets you turn the original down to save headroom. SIMLAF Sub has virtual-bass presets made for this, among them Small Speaker Bass, Laptop Bass Rescue and Phone Speaker Translation, and its new low end stays locked in phase with the original. Set its range to follow the 808, for example 40–150 Hz, and blend by ear.

Built into TECTON 2 and hVOLT

TECTON 2 has a Translation control that derives harmonics from the low notes themselves, so the pitch reads on small speakers. There is no oscillator and no added octave. Every amp hears the whole bass, fundamental included; Sub Direct then sends part of the amplified low band around the cabinet, like a direct box under the mic, and Clean Blend puts the clean signal beside the amps when you want a cleaner bottom. Its 120 amps include synth-bass and 808 families. Start with Translation low and raise it while you listen on a phone.

hVOLT rebuilds the low end of a beat, either by following its pitch or from the body of each hit, with translation harmonics, four saturation circuits and bass centring in the same chain. It is built for exactly this: 808s that have to work everywhere.

Check it like a listener

  1. Mono first. Then everything else.
  2. Use the phone itself. Export the mix, put the file on your phone and play it through the phone's own speaker at a normal listening level, not at full volume.
  3. Hum along. If you can't follow every note of the bassline, add harmonics. If the 808 turns into a buzz, pull back, or high-pass the harmonics higher.
  4. Go back to the big speakers. The sub should still be clean and no louder than before. Harmonics add peak level, so check your headroom into the limiter.
  5. Compare with a reference. Play a track you trust on the same phone, at matched loudness.

One more thing. When the kick and the 808 hit together, the 808's new harmonics can cover the kick's knock on small speakers. Shorten the overlap, or let the 808's low end duck briefly under the kick, as in the log drum lesson.
